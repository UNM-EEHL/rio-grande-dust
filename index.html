River Flow, Dust, and Health on the Middle Rio Grande

When the Rio Grande runs low through Albuquerque, more of its bed is left bare, and the people living downwind breathe more coarse particulate matter (PM10). These tools let you change how much water the river carries and see what follows for the air, for short-term mortality, and for the economic cost of that mortality.

About this project

The study follows the Middle Rio Grande from Cochiti Dam to the Isleta Diversion Dam, 64.6 miles of channel in four reaches, each with its own US Geological Survey stream gauge. Daily PM10 comes from the seven regulatory monitors in the Albuquerque area. For every monitor, reach, and day, the study counts the hours the wind blew from that reach toward that monitor, and asks whether PM10 rises more on those hours when the reach is carrying less water. It does, and the estimate rests on hours when the wind actually blew off the river, not on how close a monitor happens to be.

The change in PM10 is then carried into short-term mortality with the concentration-response functions of Liu and coauthors (2019), using monthly baseline death counts for Bernalillo and Sandoval counties, and valued at the US Environmental Protection Agency's value of a statistical life in 2015 dollars.

How the tools calculate

Every number the tools use, from the regression coefficient to the daily river flows, downwind hours, PM10, and baseline deaths, is written by the study's replication code. Your browser applies the same four equations the study does: the scenario's change in each reach's flow, the change in PM10 that follows, the change in deaths, and their value. The 95% intervals come from the same 2,000 joint draws of the regression coefficient and the mortality coefficients that the replication code writes. A test in this repository recomputes every scenario the replication code reports and checks that the browser matches it.

What the tools do not show

The results are for short-term (acute) mortality only, all ages, from PM10 only, with one set of concentration-response functions. Long-term mortality, hospital visits, and fine particulate are not included, so the dollar figures are conservative. The estimate uses same-day river flow, which understates the effect of a bed that has been drying for weeks; the study treats it as a lower bound. Scenarios far outside the flows the river actually carried from 2015 to 2024 are extrapolations.
